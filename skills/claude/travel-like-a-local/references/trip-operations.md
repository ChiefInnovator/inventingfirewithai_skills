# Booking, dining, and transport details

Use the sections relevant to the current request.

- **Make costs transparent.** For trip proposals, itemize lodging by property, room type, and night; transfers by route; activities; and agency or service fees. Show taxes, inclusions, exclusions, cancellation terms, currency, and total. Compare equivalent direct-booking options where useful. Flag unexplained markups and distinguish estimates from quotes. Willingness to pay for quality does not imply willingness to accept opaque bundles, unlimited spending, or a permanent refusal to pay a clearly justified service fee.
- **Use transfers thoughtfully.** A scenic lunch or tasting stop can enrich an already necessary transfer when it fits the route and my energy. Show the added time, detour, cost, luggage arrangements, and reservation needs. Do not turn an efficient transfer into an exhausting excursion or assume a past interest in a countryside drive overrides my current wish to relax.
- **Search booking records thoroughly.** Search all accessible relevant mail folders, including travel folders, sent messages, and archives, rather than only the inbox. Follow related correspondence and amendments. Disclose connector or folder coverage limits; a missing search result is not proof that a booking or reply does not exist.
- **Maintain one reconciled itinerary.** When asked for my plans, review the latest relevant messages and my corrections. Track each item's local date/time, location, status, source, and outstanding action. Resolve amendments, cancellations, and rebookings in sequence instead of listing each email as a separate activity. Flag conflicting records precisely. A confirmation I give you remains user-confirmed even if you cannot locate its email; say “not independently verified in the records reviewed,” not “unconfirmed.”
- **Separate communication from commitment.** Keep drafts, sent inquiries, supplier replies, availability, holds, and confirmed bookings distinct. Interest in a car, tour, or treatment is not a booking. An unanswered concierge question is still open; a draft reply has not reached the recipient. Summarize what changed and what still needs a response.
- **Compare the actual meal.** When choosing among restaurants, show current menus or useful translations, service format, representative dishes, prices, duration, and meaningful differences. When I show you a menu, recommend a specific order that fits my stated tastes and occasion. Verify which dining room and menu a reservation covers. Account for substantial afternoon tea or a long tasting menu when scheduling other meals.
- **Check every option against the actual day.** Apply closure and availability checks to alternatives as well as the first choice. A restaurant open tomorrow may be unusable tonight. For a short stay, allocate limited opportunities around operating days, existing reservations, and desired variety. Offer a feasible fallback without implying it is reserved.
- **Make hotel logistics usable.** When relevant, verify breakfast location, hours, format, and inclusion; checkout and agreed exceptions; luggage arrangements; pickup location and time; journey duration; and the next property's arrival requirements. Distinguish checkout time from vehicle pickup. Identify gaps needing a luggage or waiting plan rather than assuming they are errors. Compare an airport overnight with staying centrally only when flight timing makes that decision useful.
- **Treat remote transport as a real constraint.** Do not assume ride-hailing, taxis, or a convenient return ride will be available. Before recommending an off-property outing, verify realistic transport options, advance-booking needs, round-trip cost and duration, and the return arrangement. Compare the outing with an appealing on-property alternative. If transport cannot be verified, label that uncertainty and keep the on-property plan as the default. Avoid stacking distant activities or using all free time for excursions during a restorative stay.
- **Use destination time consistently.** Resolve “today,” “tonight,” and weekdays from my current location. Use its local time throughout the itinerary and any response timestamp; label other time zones only when they serve a purpose.
